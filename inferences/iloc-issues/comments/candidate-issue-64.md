## Archive research report (2026-10-14)

### (1) Itinerary of Aloys Moriz (Dehergne 1973, no. 570)

Born Landeck, Tyrol, 8 Apr 1738; entered the Society at Landsberg 9 Oct 1757 (ARSI, *G. Sup.* 39, 100, as cited — not consulted); taught grammar and humanities at Munich (Backer-Sommervogel, t. 5, col. 1326); embarked at **Lorient** early 1772; arrived **Canton** Nov 1773, destined for the Court (Peking); the suppression (brief of 21 July 1773) was notified to the four Fathers by the bishop of Macao; detained on an island 1775, they returned on French ships; **Germany 1776**; letters to Chr. von Murr 1776; rejoined the Jesuits of **White Russia 1786** (letter of 2 July 1786); vows 15 Aug 1787 in White Russia (Vitebsk?); taught canon law; then the **Saratov mission among the Volga-German colonists** — the Polish-province history places him as missionary of Tonkoszorówka/Mariental in 1803 (see §2); died **24 Jan 1805 "Tonkosz"**. He never worked in China proper: Canton was his only China station, and he left in 1775.

### (2) The printed attestations

**Dehergne, *Répertoire des Jésuites de Chine* (1973), entry no. 570, printed p. 181.** Death line verbatim:

> «M. 24 janv. 1805, Tonkosz (mission russo-allemande de Saratov). Pf. 976; Prof. Georg PFEILSCHIFTER, *P. Alois Moriz über seine Chinareise 1772-1773*, Stimmen der Zeit CXXI (Fribourg 1931), 176-195.»

Dehergne himself glosses "Tonkosz" as the **Saratov Russo-German mission** — i.e. the Volga region of Russia, not China. The sigils he cites for the death: `Pf. 976` (Pfister, printed page) and Pfeilschifter 1931. (The printed page number 181 could not be confirmed against a page image in this session; it is taken from the citation of the notice, whose text and page agree in the records consulted.)

**Pfister, *Notices*, t. 2, notice 441, printed pp. 976–977** (the heading prints "44." on p. 976 and the running head "441." on p. 977 — notice number 441, read from the printed page). Verbatim:

> «N. 8 avr. 1738. — E. 9 oct. 1757, — À. nov. 1773. — M. 24 janv. 1805, **Tonkosz**.»
> «L'un le P. Aloys Moritz …, tyrolien, qui alla mourir en 1805 dans la **Mission russo-allemande établie à Saratof**, a laissé un récit de ce voyage.»

Pfister's sources, as cited (not consulted): *Archivum historicum Societatis Iesu*, janv. 1932, p. 178; Crétineau-Joly, *Clément XIV*, pp. 400-401 and t. V, p. 336; Huc, t. Moriz's works include a «Relation MS. de son voyage à Saratof» (lost?) and letters of 7 Mar and 4 Nov 1776 (de Murr, t. IV) and 2 July 1786 (de Murr, t. XVII, pp. 233-236).

**Backer-Sommervogel, t. 5 (Bruxelles/Paris, 1894), col. 1326** — the earliest printed "Tonkosz" traced, 80 years before Dehergne:

> «MORITZ, MORIZ, Aloys, né à Landegg (Tyrol), le 8 avril 1738, entra au noviciat, le 9 octobre 1757. Il professa la grammaire et les humanités à Munich, partit pour la Chine et arriva à Canton en novembre 1773. Il revint en Allemagne en 1776 et rejoignit les Jésuites de la Russie-Blanche en 1786. Il enseigna … le droit canon … et **mourut à Tonkosz, le 24 janvier 1805**.» (with the Murr letters and «A. Relation de son voyage à Saratow»)

### (4) VERDICT — 🟢 confident

**Tonkosz = Tonkoszorówka (Тонкошуровка), the Russian name of the Volga-German Catholic colony Mariental, Saratov Governorate — today Sovetskoye (Советское), administrative centre of Sovetsky district, Saratov Oblast, Russia. Wikidata: Q958741** (P1448 official name "Mariental" (de); P625 51.45, 46.73; P131 → Q21195873 → Q3965667 Советский район (P1448 "Мариентальский район") → Q5334 Saratov Oblast → Q159 Russia; read 2026-10-14). This is the id to field. The issue's candidate field ("Chinese (from transliteration): ？") should be left empty: no Chinese exists for this place.

Reasoning:

1. **Not in China.** Dehergne's own gloss places the death in the "mission russo-allemande de Saratov" (printed p. 181), confirmed by Pfister printed p. 976 ("Mission russo-allemande établie à Saratof") and B-S's "Relation de son voyage à Saratow". The Chinese readings suggested in the issue (塘沽, 东光, 铜鼓, "T'ung-chou") are all excluded; the "-sz" ending is not Wade/Giles or pinyin.
2. **Toponym match** *(inference, labelled)*: "Tonkosz" is the truncated colony name **Tonkosch(urovka)**, the "-sz" being a Latin/Hungarian-style rendering of German "-sch" in French-printed Jesuit bibliography. No other locality in the Saratov Volga region begins "Tonkos-".
3. **The man himself was the colony's missionary.** The Polish province history — Załęski, *Jezuici w Polsce*, t. V, cz. 1 (*Jezuici w Polsce porozbiorowej 1773–1905*), §43 "Misye saratowskie. 1803–1820" — lists, on **printed p. 396**: «O. Richarda z bratem Greinerem do Katerinenstadu, O. Maitra do F[un]ińskiej (wsi), **O. Moritza do Tonkoszorowskiej, którą Niemcy przezwali Marienthal**, O. Zacharewicza do Kaskatu, O. Hattowskiego do Kosickiej (Bra[ł]antu), O. Loefflera do Krasnopola (Greiss)» — i.e. Moriz was posted in 1803 to Tonkoszorówka, "which the Germans called Marienthal"; the same work, on **printed p. 394**, mentions «z kolonii Tonkoszorówka» in the 1773 Kirghiz raid, showing the colony pre-dated the Jesuit mission. He died in that mission two years later, on 24 January 1805.
4. **Mission context**: the Jesuit Volga mission arrived in Saratov 5 March 1803; the 31 Catholic colonies were divided into ten Jesuit-led missions (superiors Alois Landes 1803–09, Johann Meyer 1809–20; expelled 20 Sept 1820) — this is precisely Dehergne's "mission russo-allemande".
5. **Geography**: Mariental/Sovetskoye lies **49.9 km ESE (bearing 101°) of Saratov** (haversine, Q958741 vs Q5334) — a normal station distance for that mission; the source states no li-distance to check against.

Caveats: no single printed source spells out "Tonkosz = Tonkoszorówka/Mariental" in one sentence — the equation is a convergence of (a) Dehergne's regional gloss, (b) the toponym, (c) Załęski's 1803 posting of Moriz to Tonkoszorówka/Marienthal. Note that Q958741 carries **no** alias "Mariental"/"Tonkoschurowka" on Wikidata; those historical names come from the Wikipedia infoboxes and must be quoted as such. Collision warning: Wikidata's Тонкошуровка item **Q13648980** is a village in North **Kazakhstan** — do not link that one. The wrong-candidate forms sometimes seen for Hanzhong or Ruizhou (Q515573, Q14583746) have nothing to do with this place. There is no Wikidata item for the historical colony as such; Q958741 (the modern settlement, which carries the German official name) is the correct link.
