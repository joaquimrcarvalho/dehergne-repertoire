### Chayüankow, Hukwang — Mathurin Lamathe · 🟢 confident (identified)

`jesuita-votos-local`, **1759-04-01** (`deh-mathurin-lamathe`)

- **Chinese (from transliteration)**: **茶园沟** — not 茶园口, not 茶园村 (§3)
- **Place name (Chinese)**: the valley **茶园沟**; the settlement is also named after its hill **磨盘山**; both lie inside the modern village **沈垭村**
- **Pinyin**: Cháyuángōu · Shěnyàcūn
- **Wikidata code**: **`Q14091289`** (沈垭村 Shenya village) — *replaces the candidate `Q1337942`*
- **Superior admin division**: 紫金镇 Zijin town (`Q14083121`) → 谷城县 Gucheng county (`Q1199165`) → 襄阳市 Xiangyang (`Q71284`)
- **Province**: Hubei 湖北省; the mission district given by the source, "Hukwang", is Qing **Huguang** (`Q2235155`)
- **Historical frame**: the "montagnes de Kou-tch'eng" (谷城县), called «Cévennes de la Chine»

## 1. What the source says

**Dehergne, *Répertoire des Jésuites de Chine de 1552 à 1800* (1973), entry no. 447 — "Lamathe, Mathurin [de] (franç.) P.", printed p. 142** (the vow line, verbatim from the page):

> «V. « **Tcha yuen keou, Houquam** » (Chayüankow, Hukwang), 1 avril 1759 [n'ayant pu le faire le 15 août 1758, écrit le P. Dugad], pr. (*Gal.* 24, 388). Il signe *Matthaeus* Lamathe.»

The archival reference is the primary source: **ARSI, *Gallia* 24, f. 388** — the French province's vow register, not consulted here. Dehergne prints the place in guillemets, his device for a source spelling he could not match to a known place, and adds **no characters, no gloss, no cross-reference**: he only normalises the spelling as *Chayüankow* and gives the mission district. In the printed volume the name occurs in **this entry and one other only** (§6); it is not in the volume's place index.

The rest of the entry: born 11 November 1723 in Aquitaine ("Adurensis" in the 1759 catalogue, perhaps Aire-sur-l'Adour, Landes, which the town's registers do not confirm); entered the Society 24 July 1741 at Bordeaux; priest 1752; embarked at Lorient on the *Duc de Chartres* 22 November 1754; arrived Macao 23 August 1755; at Hou-pei by November 1755; superior at Hukwang 1778; arrested at Hukwang between August 1784 and November 1785; died 3 December 1786 in the Chang-tsin of the Yüansi, Hou-pei.

**The second attestation fixes the place as a residence, not a way-stop**: **Dehergne, entry no. 853 — "Ts'ao, Maur (chin.) P.", printed p. 275**:

> «V. **26 mars 1759** c. spir., à **Tcha-yuen-keou** (*Germ.* 76, 250).»

Two final vows, five days apart, at the same house (§6).

## 2. The candidate is a false match

The candidate reads the final syllable as **口** and looks for a village named 茶园*村*; the item it lands on is a **county**, and not one named 茶园 at all. Note that the item given as the place's "Wikidata code" is the very entity named beside it as its *superior* division: **`Q1337942` = 衡山县 Hengshan County (衡阳市 Hengyang, 湖南省 Hunan)**.

- **Position**: 27.2399 N, 112.86347 E. From 紫金镇 (`Q14083121`, 32.13548 N, 111.3076 E) that is **≈565 km on a bearing of 164° — almost due south**; from the county seat of 谷城县 (`Q1199165`, 32.26591 N, 111.63413 E), **≈571 km at 168°**.
- **No administrative relation**: `Q1337942` → `Q144663` (衡阳市); the correct item `Q14091289` → `Q14083121` → `Q1199165` → `Q71284` (襄阳市). The two chains share no parent.
- **The only overlap is historical and province-sized**: both counties lay inside the Qing province of **Huguang** (`Q2235155`) — the "Hukwang" of the source. Hengshan went to Hunan and Gucheng to Hubei in the 1664 division; that is not proximity, and it is not evidence.

**Consequence: `Q1337942` must not be written into the source files.** It is a same-name trap of the kind recorded in #42 for the neighbouring station.

## 3. The name: the terminal is 沟, not 口

Dehergne romanises Chinese in a French, EFEO-derived system in which **aspiration is marked with an apostrophe**, and his own volume shows the convention: **Hankow (汉口) is written `Han-k'eou`**, with the apostrophe (printed p. 300; so throughout). The vow forms are written **without** one — `Tcha yuen keou` (p. 142), `Tcha-yuen-keou` (p. 275). In the same system 口 *kǒu* = `k'eou` (aspirated), whereas 沟 *gōu* = `keou` (unaspirated). **The terminal character is therefore 沟, and the name is 茶园沟** — which is what the Chinese sources call the valley (§5).

## 4. What the place was

**The station is described in Pfister's notice of P. Joseph Labbé** — **Pfister, *Notices biographiques et bibliographiques sur les jésuites de l'ancienne mission de Chine, 1552–1773*, t. 2, notice no. 292, printed pp. 633–636 (at p. 634)**:

> «Il est le premier Européen qui ait pénétré dans les « Cévennes de la Chine » ; il en prit possession en octobre 1731, et y retourna au mois d'août 1732.»

Labbé, quoted on the same page from the *Lettres édifiantes* (t. III, p. 464):

> «Il avait divisé ces montagnes en **huit différents quartiers**, qui ont chacun leur catéchiste.»

He had a house built for the missionary and admitted no non-Christian as a settler, «ainsi toute cette contrée ne sera habitée que par les adorateurs du vrai Dieu». By **1734** he had **600 chrétiens** in these mountains (*Lettres édifiantes*, t. III, p. 465); the districts were later raised **from eight to fourteen**, with **more than 6,000 chrétiens**, until «**la grande persécution de 1746 vint détruire presque entièrement cette admirable chrétienté**» (p. 634, from *Lettres édifiantes*, t. III, p. 780).

**Its most flourishing years are recorded under Mou-p'an-chan (磨盘山)** — **Pfister, t. 2, notice no. 325 (P. Jean-Sylvain de Neuvialle), printed p. 724**:

> «il cultiva pendant six ans les chrétientés du Hou-koang, et spécialement celle de **Mou-p'an-chan** (1740-1746), une des plus nombreuses et des plus florissantes, où il baptisa **plus de 6.000 infidèles**.»

The same page's note 2 supplies the geography: «**A sept lieues de Kou-tch'eng** vicariat actuel de « **Lao-ho-kow** » » (citing M. Cr. Chaney, *La colonie du Sacré-Cœur*, pp. 4 ff.).

**Pfister's index gathers the six missionaries of the locality under Kou-tch'eng (谷城), the "Cévennes de la Chine"** — **t. 2, *Table alphabétique*, printed p. 1049**: «Kou-tch'eng … «Cévennes de la Chine» : les PP. Labbe, 634 ; — de Neuvialle, 724 ; — Bataillé, 730 ; — Loppin, 795 ; — de la Roche, 800 ; — **de Lamathe, 883**.» Lamathe's own notice records the posting that produced the vow — **t. 2, notice no. 415, printed pp. 882–885 (at p. 883)**: «il alla, **en 1758**, au Hou-pé, dans les **montagnes de Kou-tch'eng** … pour y remplacer le P. de la Roche», with P. Maur (Ts'ao) as his colleague.

**The end of the Jesuit presence there** is in Dehergne's roster of the general persecution — **printed p. 352**: «HUKWANG : PP. **M. LAMATHE**, Louis Kao, Agost. de AVELLAR, Pierre LADMIRAL.»

## 5. Where it is today

Chinese local history names the same settlement:

- **茶园沟 is in the western mountains of 谷城县 and is now 紫金镇沈垭.** The Xiangyang municipal archives, on the proscription of 1723: «襄阳、安陆、武昌等地的天主教骨干纷纷逃到**谷城西部山区茶园沟**（**现紫金镇沈垭**）定居» (梁伶俐, 《天主教在鄂西北传播始末》, 2012).
- **磨盘山 = 茶园沟.** A parish history: «**磨盘山（又名茶园沟，今属湖北省谷城县紫金镇）**，买下整片山谷». The same account dates the first missionary, **胥孟德 (Joseph Labbé)**, to 1731–1732, describes **eight** Christian villages, a **great persecution in 1746**, and records that in **1758 the Jesuit 何弥德 came to Mopanshan** (张多默, 《为什么磨盘山称为教友谷？》, 2011). **何弥德 = Ho Mi-té** is Lamathe's own Chinese name (*Ho Mi-Té Tou-lin*): the Chinese tradition and the printed notice agree on the man and the year.
- **The site**: the church 《沈垭天主堂》 stands in **紫金镇沈垭村**, on **磨盘山** at the western edge of the **茶园沟** basin, 70 km south-west of the county seat, adjoining 房县 Fang county.

The two traditions converge on every checkable point:

| Chinese local history | Dehergne / Pfister |
|---|---|
| Labbé, first missionary, 1731–1732 | Labbé, «le premier Européen» in the "Cévennes de la Chine", possession 1731, back August 1732 |
| eight Christian villages | «huit différents quartiers» |
| some 6,000 Christians | 600 in 1734; later 14 districts and more than 6,000; Neuvialle's 6,000 baptisms, 1740–46 |
| great persecution of 1746 | «la grande persécution de 1746 …» |
| 1758: the Jesuit 何弥德 at Mopanshan | Lamathe sent to the mountains of Kou-tch'eng in 1758 |

The decisive joint is Pfister's note: **Mou-p'an-chan, seven leagues from Kou-tch'eng, at the modern vicariate of Lao-ho-kow** — the same county and the same hills as 茶园沟/磨盘山 today.

## 6. The two vow records are one place (this issue and #62)

`Tcha-yuen-keou` is the vow place of **two** men in the spring of 1759: Maur Ts'ao on 26 March (Dehergne, p. 275; issue **#62**) and Lamathe on 1 April (p. 142; **this issue**). Pfister places the two together in the Kou-tch'eng mountains in 1758 (p. 883). The identification here therefore settles **both** rows, and the same corrected value belongs on `deh-maur-tsao` as on `deh-mathurin-lamathe`.
