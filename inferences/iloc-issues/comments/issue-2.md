## 1. Where the sentence stands, and the running-head trap

**DEHERGNE, *Répertoire des Jésuites de Chine de 1542 à 1800* (1973), notice no. 407 — « Hinderer, Romain (alsacien) P. » (printed pp. 126–127).** The heading, with the notice number and the Chinese name, is on **printed p. 126**:

> « **Hinderer, Romain (alsacien) P. 407** — Henderer (JS 134, 422a), Hederer; Tô Ma-no (Pf.). N. 12 sept. 1668 … 1711, Hangchow fou, Tchô-kiang, succède à Barelli, qui vient de mou- »

— and the page breaks mid-word. The **next page (printed p. 127)** opens with the continuation and carries the Lofeu sentence:

> « …rir; 1712, chargé de dresser les cartes: Honan, 1712; Kiangnan, Chekiang, Fukien, Formose, 1713-1714 et Kiangsi, 1717; 1718, Hangchow fou; **8 sept. 1721 (ARSI FG. 730 n° 14 a. 1722, et JS 179, 131) à 1724, et de 1725 à 1729, visiteur de Chine et du Japon; 1722, Canton (JS 179, 226). Lors de l'arrestation des PP. Messari et Bucarelli au Tonkin, il va à proximité, à « Lofeu » du 28 déc. 1722 au 14 avril 1723, et y envoie le P. de Mello (JS 179, 249)**; durant la persécution de 1724, caché au Chekiang; Hangchow 1725 sq … **M. 26 août 1744 « Cham xo », Changshu (Tch'ang-chou) du Soochow fou** … **Pf. 611**; NCE (B. Lahiff). **CHANEY, Th, *Vie du P. Romain Hinderer*, Paris-Tournai, 1889** … »

**The page's running head is "HOPPE"** — and that is the trap. Dehergne gives each printed page a running head naming the **last notice to begin on that page**, not the notice at the top of it. Printed p. 127 carries two notices: **407 (Hinderer), continued from p. 126**, and **409 (Hoppe, Johann)**, which begins lower down the same page (*« N. « Silesius », Schweidnitz, 12 juill. 1708 — E. 20 oct. 1724 … P. 1735. En 1737 au Japon; 1740 en Chine? … M. v. 1781 au Tonkin »*). Three internal checks settle the attribution of the Lofeu sentence to Hinderer:

1. **The text runs on.** Page 126 ends with the half-word *« … qui vient de mou- »*; page 127 begins *« rir; 1712, chargé de dresser les cartes… »* — one continuous sentence of the same notice.
2. **The bibliography is Hinderer's**: *« Pf. 611 »* (Pfister's Hinderer notice, printed pp. 611–616), *Chaney, Vie du P. Romain Hinderer* (1889), *Platzweg, Lebensbilder*, the Formosa manuscript.
3. **Hoppe cannot be the man**: his own notice gives his entry into the Society as **20 October 1724**, so he was not in China in 1722.

**The attribution also coheres with Hinderer's dates**: he is at Canton in 1722 (*JS* 179, 226) and is *visiteur de Chine et du Japon* from 8 September 1721 to 1724 — the office that explains his forwarding a colleague into Tonkin. The colleague sent, Father **António de Melo**, has his own notice (no. 539, printed p. 172) reading *« En 1723, au Tonkin, lors de la persécution (JS 179, 249) »* — the same archival folio Dehergne cites for Lofeu.

*(One caution inside the notice, unrelated to Lofeu: the same page gives Hinderer's birth as 12 September 1668 but its quoted baptismal extract as 12 September 1658, while Pfister's notice gives 21 September 1669. The dates of notice 407 are not uniform.)*

## 2. What the other printed sources do — and do not — say

- **PFISTER, *Notices*, t. 2, notice no. 277 (*Le P. Romain Hinderer*), printed pp. 611–616.** Its only mention of Tonkin is the official formula: *« Deux fois revêtu, dans les temps les plus difficiles, en 1722 et vers 1729, des fonctions de **visiteur des missions du Japon, de la Chine et du Tonkin** (Ibid., n° 201)… »* (printed p. 614). The notice contains **no** mention of Lofeu, Messari, Bucarelli or de Mello, and its index of places (printed p. 1046) has no Lofeu.
- **SOMMERVOGEL, *Bibliothèque de la Compagnie de Jésus*, t. 4 (1893), s.v. « HINDERER, Romain », col. 393–394**, gives only *« né à … (Alsace), 1669, entra au noviciat, le 29 septembre 1686. Il arriva dans la mission de Chine en 1707 et mourut le 26 août 1744 »* — with the map-work done with Régis and de Mailla, and Chaney's biography. **No Lofeu.**
- **Chaney, *Vie du P. Romain Hinderer* (Paris–Tournai, 1889)** — the obvious place to look for a fuller narrative of the 1722–23 journey, cited in notice 407's bibliography and by Sommervogel — **is not held in this archive and was not consulted.**

## 3. What *Lofeu* is — and how far the identification goes

The name is not Dehergne's invention. In the modern scholarship on the 1722 Tonkin persecution it is written **羅浮**, and it is placed in exactly the terms Dehergne uses ("à proximité" of Tonkin):

> *1722 年官兵們終於在中越邊境越方的**羅浮** (Lofeu) 地區找到布夏萊利和梅沙利 (Jean-Baptiste Messari) 神父 … 羅浮位於中越邊境，布夏萊利神父等離開箇瑟後，得到上級指示前往中國，他們躲在荊棘濃密的樹林中，等待機會進入廣西，可惜終於被發現了。*
> — 鄭永常, *血紅的桂冠：十六至十九世紀越南基督教政策研究* (臺北: 稻鄉出版社, 2015), p. 117

("In 1722 the soldiers finally found Fathers Bucarelli and Messari … in the **羅浮 (Lofeu)** region on the Vietnamese side of the China–Tonkin border. 羅浮 lies on the China–Tonkin border; after leaving 箇瑟 (Kẻ Sặt) they had orders to go to China, hid in dense thorn forest waiting to enter **廣西 (Guangxi)**, and were discovered.") The same work names *羅浮* again as the frontier settlement on the clandestine route into Tonkin (pp. 132, 133, 135), and quotes a Nguyễn-court rescript of the 1850s in which the French and the Lê pretender meet "*at 巴汛 … or by invitation at **欽州、羅浮洞***" (p. 232) — i.e. the same frontier zone is named from the Chinese side too, near **欽州 (Qinzhou)**, in the Guangdong/Guangxi border country.

**What this establishes, and what it does not.** The characters **羅浮** and the general setting — a frontier station on the China–Tonkin border, on the road towards Guangxi — are solid, and they fit Hinderer's recorded presence at Canton in 1722 and his office as visitor. What is **not** established: whether the 1722 Jesuit 羅浮 is the 羅浮洞 of the Nguyễn records near Qinzhou or a settlement on the Tonkin side of the modern frontier, and therefore which modern administrative unit contains it. Dehergne's *« Lofeu »* is, moreover, printed in **guillemets** — his device for a *source's* spelling — and comes from **JS 179, 249** (ARSI, *Japonica-Sinica* 179, fol. 249), which is not in this archive. Treat the identification as **probable**.

## 4. Negative checks

`Lofeu` occurs in **one** document in the whole archive consulted: Dehergne's notice 407. The name does not occur in Pfister t. 2 (checked in full), in Backer–Sommervogel t. 4 (Hinderer), in Dehergne's *Les chrétientés de Chine de la période Ming* (1957), or in Dehergne 1980/1982; no transcription carries the characters 羅浮.
