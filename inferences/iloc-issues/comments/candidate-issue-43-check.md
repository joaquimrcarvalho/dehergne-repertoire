## Identification report (14 October 2026)

**Verdict: the place is identified — the vow village is 呂家莊 (Lüjiazhuang) in 束鹿縣 Shulu hien, the county immediately south of 保定府 Paoting fu (Zhili/Hopei); the contributed reading 刘家庄 is superseded.** The decisive evidence is Dehergne's own cross-reference: his notice sends the reader to *AHSI* 22 (1953), p. 326, and that printed page — §31, the list of the ancient chrétientés of Shulu hien — begins with « Lu-kia-tchoang **呂家莊** ». The vow formula itself, *« Xu le leu kia chuom »*, reads as **束鹿呂家莊** — *Shulu* (county) + *Lüjiazhuang* (village). The county is today **辛集市 Xinji** (Hebei); the village has no Wikidata item, so the anchor is the county. Confidence is high for the county and for the characters 呂家莊; only the modern village site remains open. The discrepancy between Dehergne's two romanisations (`Leou` 1973 / `Lu` 1953) is recorded in §4.

### 2. The decisive page: Dehergne 1953, *AHSI* 22, §31, printed p. 326

**Dehergne, "La mission de Pékin vers 1700 : étude de géographie missionnaire", *Archivum Historicum Societatis Iesu* 22 (1953), 314–338, §31, printed p. 326:**

> « 31. Shulu (Chou-lou) hien **束鹿**, tout au sud de Paoting et au sud-sud-est de Shenchow. D'ANVILLE: Chou-lou-hien. La mission date de la fin des Ming. Sur les anciennes chrétientés : **Lu-kia-tchoang 呂家莊**, Lao-kia-ying 駱家營, Cha-ho 沙河, Tien-kong-ying 天宮營, Pei-ying 北營, Siao-wang-ts'uen 小王村, Mu-k'ieou 木邱, Tchao-kia-tchoang 趙家莊, voir BCP, 1939, 207; 1942, 153. »

This is the page Dehergne's own notice cites for the gloss "Leou-kia-tchouang du Chou-lou hien". Three things follow:

1. **`Chou-lou hien` = 束鹿縣 Shulu hien**, the county "tout au sud de Paoting", identified by Dehergne himself, with d'Anville's map spelling *Chou-lou-hien*.
2. **The first of the ancient chrétientés of that hien is `Lu-kia-tchoang 呂家莊`** — a Catholic village, exactly the class of place a vow formula names. It is the only item in the list whose first syllable matches the notice's `Leou`/`leu` (the list's other 家莊 is 趙家莊 `Tchao-kia-tchoang`).
3. The list is an old-Ming list of Christian communities; the *BCP* (= *Bulletin catholique de Pékin*) references 1939, 207 and 1942, 153 are Dehergne's sources for it and are not consulted here.

**The chronology line for this county.** The *Répertoire*'s chronology places it on **printed p. 356**: under Chihli, *« Paoting (Tsingyüan) 1605 et les hien de Kaoyang v. 1641, **Shulu v. 1644** »* — the Répertoire's own list of old Christian communities again couples Shulu with Paoting.

### 3. The other printed attestations

1. **Dehergne 1973, entry no. 745 — printed p. 240.** The vow line, verbatim:
   > « V. «**Xu le leu kia chuom**», 7 juin 1744, c. spir.: «Joseph SARAYVA» (Lus. 24, **227** = à **Leou-kia-tchouang du Chou-lou hien**, près de Paoting fou (AHSI, 22 (1953), 326). »
   Dehergne quotes the primary register (ARSI, *Lusitana* 24, f. 227 — not consulted) and adds his own gloss: **Leou-kia-tchouang of Chou-lou county, near Paoting fou**.
2. **Dehergne 1957, «Les chrétientés de Chine de la période Ming (1581–1650)», *Monumenta Serica* 16, §12, printed p. 96:**
   > « **Shulu (Chou-lou) 束鹿 hien**, tout au sud de Paoting, à l'est de Chaochow. Inconnu de M. — Vers 1644, Shulu, et Kaocheng du Chengting fou, sont deux chrétientés fondées par deux mandarins militaires chrétiens (B. 114 citant Morelli; BCP 1942 153). »
   Dehergne's own identification, **with characters**: *Chou-lou* = **束鹿** county — an old Christian community (founded c. 1644). The article's place index repeats it: « Shulu, Chou-lou — Peking 12 » (**printed p. 133**).
3. **Pfister, notice no. 336 — printed pp. 735–736**: summary line « Coadj. spir. **1744** » with **no place**; the narrative adds nothing on the vows. The Chinese name heading is 陳聖咏.

### 4. The romanisation discrepancy — recorded, not hidden

The *Répertoire* (1973) prints **Leou**-kia-tchouang; the 1953 list prints **Lu**-kia-tchoang. In Dehergne's transliteration system (École française d'Extrême-Orient) *leou* normally renders 婁/樓 and *lu* renders 呂/盧/魯, so two readings are possible on paper:

- **呂家莊** — the village actually printed on the page Dehergne cites. The vow formula's `leu` fits this. This is the reading adopted here.
- **婁家莊** (Lóujiazhuang) — a village of the same naming pattern, which would match `Leou` exactly — but it is not attested anywhere consulted, and Dehergne would then be mis-citing his own page.

The identification is made on the citation, not on the vowel: Dehergne's notice points to *AHSI* 22 (1953), p. 326, and the 家莊 on that page whose first syllable is `Lu`/`Leou` is **呂家莊**.

**The contributed 刘家庄 reading is therefore superseded.** It rests on modern Chinese Catholic-village evidence (a 刘家庄天主堂 in present-day 辛集市), which identifies a modern Catholic village but does not come from the vow formula or from the page Dehergne cites; the characters 刘 and 呂 are different, and the 1953 list the notice cites prints 呂家莊.

### 5. Where the place is today

**Chou-lou hien = 束鹿县 Shulu county — identified by Dehergne himself** (1957, printed p. 96, characters 束鹿), under **保定府 Baoding fu** in Ming (via 祁州) and directly in Qing. Administratively the county was abolished on **5 March 1986** and replaced by **辛集市 Xinji** (county-level city, Hebei; now province-administered, formerly under 石家庄市 Shijiazhuang). Wikidata: historical county **Q113491787 束鹿縣** (P131: Q10887632 保定府 and Q113651237 深州; P1366 successor **Q1015480 辛集市**, P442 `13 01 81`). Direction check: the Xinji seat lies **106 km at 12° (SSW) of Baoding** — agreeing with Dehergne's « tout au sud de Paoting » (1957), though his 1973 « près de Paoting fou » is loose for ~100 km; the tie to Paoting is administrative (same prefecture), not topographic. The village **呂家莊 itself has no Wikidata item**, so the `.cli` link should be at the county.

### 6. What would settle it

1. **ARSI, *Lusitana* 24, f. 227** (the vow register): the original wording of «Xu le leu kia chuom» — the register may give fuller spellings or characters for the Chinese coadjutors' vow places.
2. **B. 114 (Morelli)** and ***Bulletin catholique de Pékin*** **1939, p. 207; 1942, p. 153** — the sources of Dehergne's 1953 village list; they would fix the characters of the other Shulu villages and probably give the parish name.
3. **《辛集市志》/《束鹿县志》** (religious-affairs chapter) and diocesan histories (安国教区, to which modern 束鹿/辛集 belonged) for the founding date of 呂家莊堂 — a documented 18th-c. (or earlier) foundation would close the loop.
