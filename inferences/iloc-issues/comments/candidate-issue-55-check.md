> **Verdict: the place is identified — 三橋 (Sanqiao), a market town 15 li (≈ 7.5–8.6 km) south-east of 瑞州府 Ruizhou fu, i.e. of present-day 高安 Gao'an, Jiangxi. ## 1. The source for the vow line: Dehergne no. 459

**DEHERGNE, *Répertoire des Jésuites de Chine de 1542 à 1800* (1973), notice no. 459 — « Le Febure (Lefèvre; Le Febvre), Louis-Joseph (franç.) » — printed p. 186:**

> « **« San kiao hou kia » [près de Juichow (Jouei-tcheou), Kiangsi] 19 mars 1741, pr. (Gal. 22, 59). Il signe Ludov. Joseph LEFEBURE.** … Pf. 742. Les catal. d'Aquitaine écrivent habituellement Jos. Lud. LEFEVRE. »

Dehergne's own square bracket supplies the identification-in-outline: **near Juichow (Jouei-tcheou), Kiangsi** — *Jouei-tcheou* being his spelling of **瑞州府 Ruizhou fu**. The vow formula's source is cited as *Gal.* 22, 59 (an archival series, not consulted directly here).

## 2. What "San-kiao" is: **三橋**, 15 li south-east of Ruizhou

Dehergne had already identified the place, with its Chinese characters and its distance, in his study of the Ming-period Christian communities:

> **DEHERGNE, «Les chrétientés de Chine de la période Ming (1581–1650)», *Monumenta Serica* 16 (1957), p. 70, note 11 (Kiangsi section):**
> « **JUICHOW (Joei-tcheou) 瑞州府 fou, actuel Kaoan 高安.** M. : *Xuicheu*; A. : *Choui-tcheou fou*. Un certain **胡應茂 Hou In-meou** émigre de **San-kiao 三橋 (bourg à 15 li sud-est de Juichow)** à Yangchow 揚州 du Kiangsu en 1638 … »

So, in Dehergne's own words: **瑞州府 = present-day 高安 (Gao'an)**, and **San-kiao = 三橋, a *bourg* 15 li south-east of the prefectural city**. The article's index repeats the cross-reference under the place (**"Sankiao — Kgsi 1 n. 11"**, *Table des lieux*, printed p. 133). The vow formula's place is therefore **"Sanqiao of Ruizhou"**, in Jiangxi.

**Distance.** The Qing *li* is ≈ 0.576 km, so 15 li ≈ **8.6 km**; on the modern 500 m *li* it is 7.5 km. There is no need to choose: both put the place between 7½ and 9 km from the old prefectural seat.

## 3. The household form *hou kia*

The vow formula reads *« San kiao **hou kia** »*. Read as **三橋胡家** ("Sanqiao of the Hu family"), it fits the community Dehergne describes: the San-kiao notice of 1957 concerns a **胡應茂 Hou In-meou** of San-kiao, who left for Yangzhou in 1638 and later returned there with his family. The 18th-century Christian settlement at San-kiao was thus a **Hu-family place**, and its chapel of the Blessed Virgin Mary served both Le Febvre (1741) and Tch'en (1745) as the site of their vows. **This reading is an inference:** Dehergne prints *hou kia* without characters, and no source consulted glosses it.

## 4. The same chapel, four years later

**Dehergne, notice no. 830 — « Tch'en, Pierre (chin.) » — printed pp. 303–304:**

> « **V. 8 sept. 1745, c. spir., à Choui tcheou San Kiao, chapelle de la B. Vierge Marie entre les mains du P. LEFEBURE. Il signe : Petrus CHIN, avec son nom chinois ; de même, le P. LEFEBURE (Gal. 28, 191).** »

That is: the Chinese Jesuit **Pierre Tch'en** (*« Petrus Sina »*, Chinese name 陳多祿 Tch'en To-lo) took his vows as *coadjutor spiritualis* on **8 September 1745** in the **chapel of the Blessed Virgin Mary at 三橋 (San-kiao) of 瑞州 (Choui-tcheou)**, into the hands of **Le Febvre** — the same priest who had made his own profession in that chapel on 19 March 1741. Dehergne adds that *Franc.* 21, 339 wrongly places Tch'en's vows at **Pékin** in 1745; Pfister's notice of Tch'en is **no. 340**, printed **pp. 738–739**, and of Le Febvre **no. 345**, printed **p. 742** (which is exactly how Dehergne cites him: « Pf. 742 »). Pfister's notice of Tch'en also identifies the "**Pater Petrus Sina**" whom Bahr, Gogeisl and Hallerstein met at "Xa-hu" in Kiangsi on 27 April 1739 as Tch'en, "chargé d'une chrétienté au Kiang-si", and notes that Tch'en was Le Febvre's companion in the persecution of 1746 (printed p. 739, notes 2 and 3).

Pfister's own *Table alphabétique* gives the same page range for Tch'en — « TCH'EN (Le P. Pierre) — «Chin», «Petrus Sina», n° 340, pp. 738, 739 » (printed p. 1094).

## 5. Where it is today, and the Wikidata correction

- **瑞州府** was abolished with the prefecture system (1912–13). Its seat, **高安**, is now the county-level city **高安市, 宜春市 Yichun, 江西省 Jiangxi** (`Q1229428`).
- **15 li south-east of the old prefectural city** points to the south-east of modern Gao'an, where the village **三桥村** lies in **荷岭镇 Heling town, 高安市** — the modern administrative record places Sanqiao village exactly there.
- **The proposed Wikidata code must change.** The candidate block gives **Q14583746**, which is **瑞州街道 (Ruizhou Subdistrict)** — the urban sub-district of 高安市, administrative code `36 09 83 001`. The right item is **[Q14583988](https://www.wikidata.org/wiki/Q14583988) — 三桥村 (Sanqiao village)**, whose administrative-division code **P442 `36 09 83 101 203`** nests it under 荷岭镇 Heling town (`36 09 83 101` → `Q14583980`) and 高安市 (`36 09 83` → `Q1229428`): province **36** Jiangxi, prefecture **09** Yichun, county **83** Gao'an, town **101** Heling, village **203** Sanqiao. Q14583988 says it is located in Heling town, and Heling town's *contains* (P150) list includes Q14583988.
- **Distance check.** Heling town's coordinate (28.35111 N, 115.41594 E) is **9.0 km** from Gao'an's own coordinate (28.42222 N, 115.37222 E), on a bearing of **152° (south-south-east)** — Dehergne's "15 li south-east" is ≈ 8.6 km on the Qing *li*. Direction and distance agree to within the rounding of a gazetteer entry. (Q14583988 itself carries **no coordinate**, so this measures to the town centre, not to the village land.)

**Caveats, stated plainly.** (a) The equivalence of today's 三桥村 with the 18th-century San-kiao rests on **name + direction + distance**, not on a local source naming the Christian community or the chapel; the chapel's exact site is not established. (b) Wikidata holds **many** items labelled 三桥村 (at least twenty in a first search page alone), nearly all "village in China"; Q14583988 is picked out **solely** by its P131/P442 chain, so anyone re-using the code should re-check that chain. (c) Dehergne's 1957 article itself classes the Ming-period Christianity of Ruizhou among the traditions "qui n'emportent pas pleine conviction"; the San-kiao chapel is documented only from the 18th-century vow formulas.
