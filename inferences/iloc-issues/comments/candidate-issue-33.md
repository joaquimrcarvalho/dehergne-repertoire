### Ko-li-tsem do Kiating, Kiangnan — Dionísio Ferreira  ·  🟡 tentative — please verify

`estadia`, **1753-12-08** (`deh-dionisio-ferreira`)

Same place and same fields as the 1745 record — this second line is the day he was **arrested there**, not a second residence (§2).

## 1. What the source says

**Dehergne, *Répertoire des jésuites de Chine de 1552 à 1800* (1973), notice no. 300 — "Ferreira, Dionisio (Denis) (port.)" — printed p. 91:**

> «… Emb. 1745(?) (W 1908: Dinis FERREIRA) arr. 1745 (JS 134, 446 et non 1746 (JS 134, 439, à tort); **1745, réside à « Ko-li-tsem » du Kiating, Kiangnan (M 435, 362); il y sera capturé 8 déc. 1753** (M 445, 245-248, cf. 271-272 et 319-360); exil à Macao, 1756; arrêté là, le 5 juill. 1762, Fort St Julien, Lisbonne, du 19 oct. 1764 au 6 sept. 1767; déportation en Italie. […] M. 22 mars 1771, Castel Gandolfo près de Rome (HS 53a, 153; Lus. 41, p. 193). **Pf. 830 ignore son nom chinois.**»

One sentence supplies both issue records: he **resided** there in 1745, and he was **captured there** on 8 December 1753. The rest of the notice is the Portuguese province's record of the rest of his life: born Lisbon 9 or 10 October 1720; entered the Society 5 February 1738; embarked 1745 (Wicki's list no. 1908, *Dinis Ferreira*); arrived 1745; final vows at Macao 3 May 1756 (*Lus.* 17, 222); arrested there 5 July 1762 by Pombal's officers; imprisoned in Fort St Julien, Lisbon, 19 October 1764 – 6 September 1767; relegated to Italy; died at Castel Gandolfo 22 March 1771.

**Pfister (1932–34), t. 2, notice no. 382, p. 830 — no locality:**

> «Ce Père, portugais de nation, né à Lisbonne, arrivé en Chine en 1745, exerça son apostolat dans la province du Kiang-nan, **selon toutes les probabilités**, car il fut arrêté en 1753, avec P. de Araujo et trois autres compagnons, torturé en 1754 et enfin exilé à Macao en 1756.»

Pfister's notice gives no village, no station and no Chinese name; Dehergne's «Ko-li-tsem» is therefore the **only** naming of the place in the printed literature consulted. The persecution itself is told in Pfister, t. 2, p. 775 (the 1755 intercession for the *«prisonniers de Nankin»*), pp. 863–864 (de Araujo) and p. 870 (Emmanuel de Viegas), and printed in the *Lettres édifiantes*, éd. Panthéon, t. IV, pp. 42–43.

## 2. The two records are one place, twice

The `estadia` 1745 entry is the residence; the `estadia` 1753-12-08 entry is the arrest at that same residence. Dehergne's *«il y sera capturé»* — "he will be captured **there**" — binds the second date to the place named in the first clause. Editing consequence: the two records must carry the same place value and the same (unknown) Chinese form; if the locality is ever resolved, both lines change together.

## 3. The name `Ko-li-tsem`, and where Dehergne took it

**It is quoted, not identified.** Dehergne prints it in guillemets — « Ko-li-tsem » — the same device he uses elsewhere for a source's own spelling that he could not match to a known place. Nothing in the notice, and nothing in Pfister, glosses it.

**The source is the Paris Foreign Missions archive.** In his abbreviations (printed p. XXXIV) Dehergne defines **M** as «M. (MEP., AMEP.) Archives des Missions Étrangères de Paris». His citation **M 435, 362** therefore = **AMEP, vol. 435, p. 362**. The volume can be identified from the MEP's own published finding aid (the *table analytique* of the AMEP, digitised by IRFA, Paris): its opening leaf is headed «**Volume 435 / Chine X - / 1747-1748**», and its leaflets are handwritten summaries keyed to the volume's own page numbers in a right-hand column. Two consequences: (a) the statement about the **1745** posting rests on a document written in **1747–48** (a report or journal of those years, looking back); (b) the finding aid is **selective** — it lists the volume's pieces by their opening page, and the page Dehergne cites (**p. 362**) carries no separate entry in it, so the aid repeats no spelling and cannot resolve *Ko-li-tsem*. Dehergne cites the same volume again at **p. 981** (*Répertoire*, printed p. 199), which shows it runs to about a thousand pages; p. 362 is early in it.

**Reading the transliteration.** The form is in the Portuguese-keyed romanisation of the vice-province (the same notice writes his Chinese name *Fei Tüô-ni*), in which a final **-m** renders the velar nasal (cf. *Pequim* 北京, *Nanquim* 南京). In the local Wu pronunciation of the Shanghai–Jiading plain the syllable written **-tsem** answers to either **村** (village) or **鎮** (market town): /tsʰəŋ/ ~ /tsəŋ/. The name is therefore *"the village / market town of **Ko-li**"*, where *Ko-li* is a two-syllable place name — the possible Chinese sequences include 高麗, 古里, 顧里, 葛里 — **but the characters are not determined by any evidence consulted**, and the candidate block's "Chinese (from transliteration): ？" is correct as it stands.

## 4. *Kiating* = 嘉定: what "of Kiating" fixes

- **What it was in 1745.** 嘉定縣, a county of the Jiangnan plain: Ming 蘇州府; 弘治十年 (1497) part of it was detached to 太倉州; under the present dynasty, **雍正三年 (1725) 改隸太倉州, 析東境置寶山縣** ("in Yongzheng 3 the county was transferred to Taicang and its eastern district was detached to form Baoshan"). So in 1745 the county was **江蘇省太倉直隸州嘉定縣**, measuring «東西廣五十四里，南北袤五十三里» (2 *li* ≈ 1 km: about 27 km east–west and 26 km north–south). Both statements stand in the county gazetteer, 卷二〈疆域志·建置〉 and 〈里至〉; on its **建置** the change is placed in 雍正三年, though the transfer is conventionally dated 1724, and the printed sources consulted do not settle which is right — the date matters here only because the eastern towns left the county with 寶山.
- **Caution on "east".** Because 寶山縣 was carved out of Kiating's east (1724/25), a village described as "of Kiating" may lie today in 嘉定區 **or** in 寶山區 (the eastern towns — 羅店, 月浦, 大場, 真如, 江灣, 高橋, 楊家行 — still appear in the Kiating gazetteer's town list, historically).
- **What it is now.** **Q662101 — 嘉定區 Jiading District, 上海市** (P131 → Q8686 Shanghai since 1958; P625 31.337447 N, 121.233592 E; P1448 嘉定区; aliases in English include *Kiating*, *Kia-ting*, *Kiatung*, in Latin *Kiating*). The Qing county has its own item, **Q46539546 (嘉定縣)**; **Q10940997 (太倉直隸州)** is the Qing parent recorded on Q662101 (for 1724–1911). Note that Q46539546's own P131 currently points at **Q30941237 (滬海道)**, the *Republican-era* circuit, not at 太倉州 — one more reason to anchor at the district item and to leave the village link empty.
- **Dehergne's own usage confirms the equation.** He writes both *Kiating* and *Kiatim* for the same place (*Répertoire*, pp. 179, 399), and in his 1957 study of the Ming Christian communities he devotes an entry to it: «**21. Kiating (Kia-ting) 嘉定** hien … En dial.: **Cadin, Kadine** … Ecrit: … **Xiatim** … **Sciatin** … **Quiatin, Kiatin** … **Ciantim** …» (1957, p. 66). That is exactly the range of spellings from which the *Ko-li-tsem* notice's «Kiating» comes.
- **A station there is unremarkable.** The Society's Kiating residence is documented from the 1610s–1620s (the church from 1617; a residence from 1621, founded by Lazare Cattaneo with Alvare Semedo and the brother François de Lagea; a language school for new missionaries in 1625; the 1627–28 conference on the Chinese terms for God), and the community was still substantial in the 18th century — **3,000 Christians at Kia-ting in 1703** (Pfister's index, t. 2, p. 1045). The 1701 residence list carries it: «Taitsang (tcheou indép.) R'; avec **Kiating R**; et Tsungming R' (9 églises)» (*Répertoire*, printed p. 358). A missionary residing in a *village* of that district in 1745 fits the pattern exactly.

## 5. Negative check: it is not one of the county's market towns

The **嘉定縣志, （清）程國棟 修, 卷二〈疆域志·市鎮〉** (Qianlong-period recension; the copy consulted is the National Library of China scan, id 311998005930, whose full text is published at shidianguji.com — see **Sources** below) lists the county's towns, markets, 行 and the few villages it records, by quarter. The text reads, verbatim:

> 市鎮 — **南境**：南翔鎮、紀王廟鎮、柵橋鎮、封家浜市、諸翟巷村、馬陸村。**北境**：婁塘鎮、唐家行、芝村。**西境**：外岡鎮、葛隆鎮、方泰鎮、安亭鎮、黃渡鎮、錢門塘市、望仙橋市、陸家行、白鶴村。**東境**：新涇鎮、徐家行鎮、吳家行、羅店鎮、月浦鎮、廣福鎮、大場鎮、真如鎮、江灣鎮、楊家行鎮、高橋鎮、劉家行、陳家行、蔣家行、趙家行、殷家行、黑橋村。

**No name in that list answers to *Ko-li-tsem*.** The recapitulation that follows the list is a record of administrative promotion rather than a second place list: seven towns of long standing (**南翔、安亭、黃渡、羅店、大場、江灣、清浦，一名高橋**), markets raised to towns (**紀廟、婁塘、新涇、廣福、真如**), 行 raised to towns (**徐家行、楊家行**), later additions (**外岡、葛隆、月浦**), markets unchanged (**柵橋、方泰**), and the 村 望仙橋 raised — as the text puts it in its own terse clauses: *「南翔、安亭、黃渡、羅店、大場、江灣、清浦，一名高橋。凡鎮七，歷年已久，市易為鎮者五。紀廟、婁塘、新涇、廣福、真如行易為鎮者二。徐家行、楊家行舊添者三。外岡、葛隆、月浦新添者二，柵橋、方泰市如故者二，封家濱、錢門塘、楊志以後不載者二。州橋、瓦浦新添者一，望仙橋為村者五，悉如故。行如故者六。」* It too yields no *Ko-li-tsem*. **The honest conclusion is that the residence was a village or an oratory site, not one of the county's towns** — with the caveat that this chapter is a *market-town and village* list and an ordinary hamlet need not appear in it. The check excludes the towns; it does not exclude every village.

## 6. What remains open, and what would settle it

- **The decisive document is AMEP, série «Chine», vol. 435 («Chine X», 1747–1748), p. 362** — the page Dehergne read. A photograph or scan from IRFA/MEP (Paris) would give the original spelling, the surrounding sentence (whose mission, whose report) and any second, fuller form of the name. Nothing in the printed literature consulted repeats it.
- **A Chinese gazetteer or 地名志** for 嘉定 (and, because of the 1724 division, 寶山 and 太倉) searched for a village named 高麗 / 古里 / 顧里 / 葛里 + 村 or 鎮. The county gazetteer's 鄉都 chapter will not help: it names only 都/區/扇/圖/圩, not places.
- **His Chinese name is a weak search key.** Dehergne gives the romanisation *Fei Tüô-ni* (read 費多尼 — **not** verified as characters); his notice says expressly that Pfister's notice 382 has no Chinese name, and Pfister's own addenda (t. 2, p. 42*) add, from the Chinese Jesuit author Siao (Siao II, 111), only «… **FEI** 費 dit Siao …» — the remaining characters are blank in the copy consulted, so the name cannot be reconstructed from it. A Chinese-language search is better started from the place.
- **Do not read Q662101 as the residence.** The district item is the modern successor of the county; the `estadia` itself is an unnamed village inside it. Leave the place's `wikidata_id` empty until the village is identified.
