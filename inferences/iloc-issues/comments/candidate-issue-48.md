## Identification report (14 October 2026)

**Verdict: the county is identified and the village is named by the sources.** "Sinfeng" is **信豐縣 (Xinfeng hien, pinyin Xìnfēng)**, a county of **贛州府 Kanchow (Ganzhou) prefecture**, southern Jiangxi; the **Wikidata anchor is [Q1356667](https://www.wikidata.org/wiki/Q1356667)** (Xinfeng County). « Pe-chieh » is the village of that county which Dehergne himself writes **白家, "ou mieux 白加" — romanised *Pé-kié / Pé-kia***, the Christian village of the Kanchow residence where **Adrien Grelon died on 3 March 1696**. The village has **no Wikidata item**, so the id belongs on the county. The reading proposed in the issue file — 新淦 / 新干 (Xin'gan, 臨江府) — is **not** the place (see §4).

### 2. What the sources say

1. **Dehergne 1973, *Répertoire*, entry no. 390, printed p. 119.** Death line, verbatim: *« M. 3 mars 1696 à « Pe-chieh » du Sinfeng, Kiangsi (JS 116, 248 et 167, 287). Sur cette chrétienté qu'il a fondée en 1691, voir AHSI 36 (1967), 230. »* — Pe-chieh is thus a **chrétienté (rural Christian community) founded by Grelon in 1691**, located *du* ("of/in") Sinfeng. The primary references are ARSI shelfmarks (JS = *Japonica-Sinica* 116, f. 248 and 167, f. 287), **not consulted**. The entry continues on printed p. 120 (end of entry: *« Pf. 295; Streit V n° 2369… »*).
2. **Dehergne, "La Chine centrale vers 1700. III. Les vicariats apostoliques de l'Intérieur (fin) : Le Kiangsi. Étude de géographie missionnaire", *Archivum Historicum Societatis Iesu* 36 (1967), fasc. 72, printed p. 230, entry 14** — the study Dehergne's own notice cites for this chrétienté. It gives both the county and the village, with characters: *« 14. Sinfeng 信豐 (Sin-fong) hien du Kanchow, au sud dudit. Distinct de 2 autres Sinfeng (non hien) au Kiangsu et au Chekiang; et de 3 Sinfengchen … »*; and, on the same page: *« Chrétienté : 白家 ou mieux 白加 Pé-kié (en dialecte), Pé-kia, appelé aussi Pé-kia-tsuen (Gabiani 63, 534), Pé-kia-pao (JS 128; WB n° 86, 24) … **Adrien Greslon meurt le 3 mars 1696 à Pé-chieh du Sin-feng** (JS 166, 248-249; 167, 287-291). »* — This article is **not in the PHA archive**; it was read on the Internet Archive scan of the volume (see Sources), which is why the printed page is given explicitly here.
3. **Pfister, *Notices*, t. 1, notice no. 104 (Greslon), printed pp. 295–297.** Printed p. 296: *« Il passa de là au Kiang-si, où il fut chargé de la résidence de Kan-tcheou fou 赣州. **Les chrétientés de Ki-ngan 吉安 et du bourg de «Pé-kia» (même province), et de T'ing-tcheou 汀州 (au Fou-kien), en dépendaient.** »* (source cited there: Greslon, *Histoire*, pp. 79 seq.). So Pfister knows a **bourg «Pé-kia» in Kiangsi** whose chrétienté depended on the Kanchow residence — **no Chinese characters given**. Printed p. 297: *« il demeura au Kiang-si jusqu'à sa mort, arrivée vers la fin de 1695 à Kan-tcheou fou; nous ignorons le jour »* — Pfister places the death at Kan-tcheou fou, late 1695; Dehergne's date and place (3 Mar 1696, Pe-chieh du Sinfeng) supersede it on the strength of the JS catalogues.
4. **Pfister, *Notices*, t. 1, notice no. 225 (Amiani), printed p. 491**: *« …puis au Kiang-si, où il demeura deux ans à Kan-tcheou fou 赣州, Ting-tcheou (3) et **Sin-fong 信豐** »* — the only Chinese characters for the county in Pfister: **信豐**. Editor's note there: Ting-tcheou 汀州 is in Fou-kien; in Kiangsi there is a Ting-nan 定南 *« précisément situé dans l'ancienne préfecture de Kan-tcheou fou »* — i.e. these stations all sat in the old Ganzhou prefecture.
5. **Pfister, *Notices*, t. 2, Table alphabétique, printed p. 1087**: place index *« Sin-fong 信豐 (Kiang-si) : les PP. Amiani, 491; — J.-P. Simonelli, 659. »* And printed p. 1070: *« «Pé-kia» (au Kiang-si), 296 »* — Pfister's own place index lemmatises the bourg, pointing to p. 296 (finding 3).
6. **Pfister, *Notices*, t. 2, notice no. 305 (Simonelli), printed p. 659**: *« …à Tch'ang-chou (Kiang-nan) en déc. 1731, **à Sin-fong (Kiang-si) en oct. 1735**, à Kan-tcheou fou (Kiang-si) en 1739, à Macao le 25 nov. 1741 »* — the Sin-fong station was still staffed in 1735.
7. **Dehergne 1973, *Répertoire*, "Carte des résidences de Chine en l'année 1701", printed pp. 357–358.** Legend (p. 357): *« R = Résidence SJ… R' = Résidence inoccupée »*; table (p. 358), under KIANGSI: *« Kanchow collège SJ; R OFM; et Ningtu R' OFM; **Sinfeng R'**; Kian R OFM; et Tingnan R OSA »* — Sinfeng was an unoccupied Jesuit residence in 1701 attached to the Kanchow college. Note the striking parallel with finding 4 (Kan-tcheou fou + Ting-nan + Sin-fong).
8. **Dehergne 1957, *Les Chrétientés de Chine de la période Ming (1581-1650)*, *Monumenta Serica* 16** — no attestation of Sinfeng/Pe-chieh/Pé-kia (the foundation post-dates 1650).
9. **Backer–Sommervogel, *Bibliothèque de la Compagnie de Jésus*, t. 3, cols 1737–1738**: *« GRESLON, GRELON, Adrien … partit de Lisbonne le 25 mars 1654, pour la Chine, y arriva en 1657, et **mourut en mars 1697, à Kan-tchou-fou** »* — again only the prefecture, and a third death date.
10. **The project's own** `sources/dehergne-locations-1701.cli:322` already fields `geo3$Sinfeng#Xinfeng 信丰, @wikidata:Q1356667/tchou-hien/…` for the 1701 residence list — consistent with the county anchor proposed here.

### 4. VERDICT — 🟢 county and village named; 🟡 modern village unlocated

- **The county anchor is solid.** "Sinfeng" = **信豐 / 信丰, Xinfeng county**, then under Ganzhou prefecture (赣州府; cf. Pfister p. 491 pairing Sin-fong with Kan-tcheou fou 赣州). Dehergne's own 1701 residence list (p. 358) confirms a Jesuit station at Sinfeng in the Kanchow group. Modern chain: 信丰县 · 赣州市 Q363166 · 江西省 Q57052. **Q1356667 is the right item for the county** (P131 = Q363166 Ganzhou, P442 = `36 07 22`; Ganzhou's P150 contains Q1356667; the item carries coordinate 25.288, 114.902). Historical item: 赣州府 **Q15449452**.
- **The village is named by Dehergne himself**: **白家, "ou mieux 白加" — Pé-kié / Pé-kia** (Dehergne, *AHSI* 36 (1967), p. 230). Pfister independently calls it the **bourg de «Pé-kia»** (t. 1, p. 296) and lemmatises it in his index (t. 2, p. 1070). The equation **Pé-kia = « Pe-chieh »** is a tight inference from Dehergne's two texts — same person (Grelon), same day (3 March 1696), same district (Sin-feng), same formula "à X du Sin-feng" — and no source gives the village any other name.
- **What stays open** is only the *modern* identification: no stated distance or direction, so no coordinate check is possible, and Wikidata has no 白家村 / 白加村 item inside Xinfeng county. The 新淦/新干 (Xin'gan, 臨江府) reading proposed in the issue file is excluded phonetically: *Sin-* = 信 (xìn) and *-feng* = 豐 (fēng), whereas 淦 is *kan*.
- **Recommendation for the field:** link the `morte` (and the 1691 `jesuita-tarefa`) line to **Q1356667 as county-level anchor only** — i.e. `ls$morte/« Pe-chieh » du Sinfeng, Kiangsi#Pe-chieh = 白家/白加 (village sem item Wikidata); ancorado ao condado @wikidata:Q1356667/16960303` — and keep the modern village unresolved. Pfister's competing death place (Kan-tcheou fou, fin 1695, p. 297) is superseded but worth an `obs=`.

### 5. What would settle it

1. **ARSI, JS 116, f. 248 and JS 167, f. 287** — the catalogue entries behind the death record; they may give a fuller romanisation of the village.
2. **信豐縣志 (Xinfeng county gazetteer, Qing editions)** — list of market towns/villages (村/墟) for a 白家/白加-type seat with a recorded early Christian community.
3. **Gabiani, *Incrementa* (63, 534)** and the **Welt-Bott (WB) n° 86, 24, 26** — the forms Pé-kia-tsuen / Pé-kia-pao, cited by Dehergne in the 1967 article; not consulted.
