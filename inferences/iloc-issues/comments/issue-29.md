## 1. What the source says

**DEHERGNE, *Répertoire des Jésuites de Chine de 1542 à 1800* (1973), notice no. 447 — « Lamathe, Mathurin [de] (franç.) P. » — printed pp. 141–142; the death line is on printed p. 142:**

> «… V. « Tcha yuen keou, Houquam » (Chayüankow, Hukwang), 1 avril 1759 [n'ayant pu le faire le 15 août 1758, écrit le P. Dugad], pr. (Gal. 24, 388). Il signe Matthaeus Lamathe. **M. 3 déc. 1786 dans le Chang-tsin du Yüansi, Hou-pei (M 448, 94; RBS 436). 137 dit: à Shan-king-kia.** N. « Adurensis », dit le catal. miss. Sinens. PP Gallorum 1759. Serait-ce Aire-sur-l'Adour (Landes)? …»

The rest of the notice: Chinese name **Ho Mi-Tô Tou-lin** (Pfister; *Tou-lin* = *Ma-tou-lin*, Mathurin); the tomb name *Wan* / *Ouan*; born 11 November 1723 in Aquitaine; entered 24 July / 14 September 1741 at Bordeaux; embarked on the *Duc de Chartres* at Lorient on 22 November 1754; arrived at Macao 23 August 1755; in Hou-pei from November 1755; superior at Hukwang in 1778; arrested between August 1784 and November 1785. The notices *immediately before* 447 (no. 446) and the vow line above show Dehergne's citation shorthand in action: **M** = AMEP (Archives des Missions Étrangères de Paris), so *M 448, 94* = AMEP vol. 448, p. 94; **RBS** (his OCR siglum for *RSB*) = *Renseignements du Bureau Sinologique de Zi-ka-wei*, 1927–1950, **numbers 1–462** — a serial number, not a page (printed p. XXXV). The bare **"137"** after the death line is the page of a work whose author's name the printed digest leaves to context; the only full account of the Hubei mission in Dehergne's bibliography is **Gubbels, *Trois siècles d'apostolat : histoire du Catholicisme au Hukwang … 1587–1870* (Paris–Wuchang, 1934)** — the work he cites in the preceding notice as « Gubbels, 154-170 » (printed p. 142).

## 2. `Yüansi` = 鄖西 (Yunxi)

- **The syntax.** Dehergne writes *« dans le **Chang-tsin du Yüansi** »* — "in the Chang-tsin **of** the Yüansi", the same construction he uses for counties and prefectures throughout (cf. *« Changshu (Tch'ang-chou) **du** Soochow fou »*, printed p. 127, and *« dans les montagnes **de** Kou-tch'eng »* in Pfister, below). *Yüansi* is therefore the **county**, and *Chang-tsin* a place inside it.
- **The name.** Wade-Giles **Yün-hsi** = 鄖西; Dehergne's *Yüansi* is the same name written unhyphenated. 鄖西縣 was a county of **鄖陽府 Yunyang fu** in Qing Hubei (湖廣/湖北, the mission district Dehergne calls *Hou-pei* and, for the same man, *Hukwang*). It is today **鄖西縣, 十堰市 Shiyan, 湖北省 Hubei**.
- **The setting agrees.** Pfister puts Lamathe from 1758 "au Hou-pé, **dans les montagnes de Kou-tch'eng**" (谷城, printed p. 883) — the same Hubei upland country, 谷城 being the county immediately east of 鄖西 — and says he remained in the Ho-nan/Hou-pé provinces and died in the latter (printed p. 884). Lamathe's vows had been at **Chayüankow, Hukwang** (茶园沟, in 谷城縣, printed p. 142), so his whole known geography is this 鄖陽–襄陽 mountain block.
- **Honesty about the step.** No page consulted *prints* 鄖西 for *Yüansi*: the archive's copy of the *Répertoire* drops all Chinese characters in OCR, and no other transcription in the archive contains the string. The equation is a one-step romanisation correspondence (Yün-hsi → Yüansi) supported by the syntax and the mission geography, and it is the **only** Hubei county whose name answers to it. Treat it as an **identification**, not as a quotation.

## 3. Why `Chang-tsin` stays unresolved

- **It is a bare romanisation, quoted rather than identified.** Dehergne uses no guillemets for it (contrast his treatment of *« Lofeu »* in notice 409), but he supplies **no Chinese characters**, no district, no modern equivalent, and — unlike hundreds of other localities in the *Répertoire* — no cross-reference.
- **It occurs nowhere else in the printed literature consulted.** A full-text search of the whole PHA archive library for `Chang-tsin`, `Shan-king-kia`, `Yüansi` and `Yunsi` returns **one page only**: Dehergne printed p. 142. Pfister's notice of Lamathe (printed pp. 882–885) gives **no locality** beyond "au Hou-pé" and the mountains of 谷城, and does not contain either name. Dehergne's 1957 study of the Ming Christian communities has a *Hukwang* chapter (printed pp. 47 ff.) but treats only Ming places — no Yunyang, no Yunxi, no Chang-tsin; his 1980 and 1982 studies are similarly Ming-period.
- **The Chinese characters cannot be recovered from the archive.** No transcription in the archive carries 鄖西 or any candidate characters for *Chang-tsin*; the archive holds **no Hubei gazetteer** (no 鄖西縣志, no 鄖陽府志, no provincial 地名志) and **does not hold Gubbels 1934**, so the source Dehergne took `Shan-king-kia` from was not consulted directly.
- **`Shan-king-kia` is not an improvement.** Gubbels' place-name (via Dehergne, printed p. 142 — "137 dit : à Shan-king-kia") is a French-keyed romanisation of a three-syllable place-name, possibly the same locality under another reading, but **no characters can be assigned to it either**. Recording it as an alternative is honest; adopting it as *the* place would not be.
