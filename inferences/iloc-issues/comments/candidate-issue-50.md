### 4. VERDICT — 🟢 confident: 柏泉 (Baiquan), today 柏泉街道, 东西湖区, 武汉市, 湖北; Wikidata anchor Q130298171

1. **The characters come from an author-side identification, not a guess.** The editor of the standard Pfister edition (printed p. 745, footnote 1) identifies the station as **柏泉 near 茅廟集**, citing the Annuaire Planchet for 1929, p. 239. 茅庙集 (Maomaoji) is the historic market town adjacent to 柏泉 in **Dongxihu District, Wuhan** — the identification and the candidate coincide.
2. **Dehergne's own gloss fits.** « à 30 Kms de Hankow » (printed p. 142): the Baiquan church item Q130298171 (30.7294 N, 114.1365 E) lies **22–23 km NW (bearing ≈ 318°)** of the old Hankou bund (Jianghan Guan), i.e. about 30 km by the road that skirts the Dongxihu lakes — agreement to road-distance rounding. The candidate is on the right side of Hankou for a station in the Han-river corridor.
3. **Phonetics (inference, labelled).** 柏泉山 *bǎiquán shān* maps onto all four spellings: 泉 *quán* = Dehergne's *-tsiuen / -tsien* (the -ü- of *ts'iüan* dropped, -n/-m and -ouan/-ien endings unstable in his romanisation); 山 = *chan* (and *schan* in the mouth of the Austrian Laimbeckhoven, who pronounced his own vows); 柏, read [pə] in the Wuhan area (Mandarin *bǎi*), gives *Pe*. No homophone in the Hankou area fits the spellings as well.
4. **The geography of all four men is coherent.** Laimbeckhoven was stationed at **Wuchang fu** (across the river from Hankou) in 1739–1743 and vowed there 2 Feb 1740; **Dugad's charge was the Christians of Hankou itself** and the boats; **Desrobert's district ran up the Han river from Hankou**; **Baron** was in Hou-kouang under the P. de la Roche in 1771. A station 30 km from Hankow, with an oratory, used for vows in 1737–1740 and again in 1771, is exactly what 柏泉 was: the mother parish of the Wuhan area.
5. **Wikidata caveats.** Q130298171 is **柏泉天主堂 (Catholic Church of Baiquan)** — a church building (P31 Q16970), P131 → Q936443 东西湖区 (P442 `42 01 12`) → Q11746 武汉市 (P442 `42 01`) → Hubei; chain verified both ways (Q936443's P150 contains 柏泉街道 Q11108336); coordinate present. **There is no item for 柏泉山 (the mountain) nor for a Dongxihu 柏泉村**; the two 柏泉村 items (Q14769407 Shandong, Q14595126 Jiangxi) and 柏泉社区 Q126009803 (Hainan) are collisions to avoid. The standing church postdates the Jesuits; linking the vow events to it anchors the **locality**, not the 1740 building. Per the brief, the id to field is **Q130298171** (locality anchor; Q11108336 柏泉街道 if a sub-district entity is preferred).

Loose ends for the `.cli` (none of the four lines carries `@wikidata:`): `dehergne-b.cli:341` (Baron), `dehergne-d.cli:360` (Desrobert), `dehergne-d.cli:1082` (Dugad), `dehergne-l.cli:310` (Laimbeckhoven) — all four may take `# @wikidata:Q130298171` (optionally noting 柏泉山，东西湖区，武汉 in the comment). Also worth recording: Pfister's vows date for Laimbeckhoven («P. 1736», before embarkation) vs Dehergne's 2 Feb 1740 at Pe tsien schan.

### 5. What would settle it

1. **Annuaire Planchet pour 1929, p. 239** — the source behind the Pfister editor's footnote; it should print the characters and the 1920s parish history of Baiquan.
2. ***Lettres édifiantes et curieuses*, t. II (éd. Panthéon), pp. 776–800** — Desrobert's and Dugad's letters (the 1741 letter is dated « de Pe-tsiuen-chan »): descriptions of the station, its oratory and its distance from Hankou.
3. **ARSI, *Lusitania* 15, f. 421; *Gallia* 21, f. 141; *Gallia* 24, f. 557** — the three vow formulas; they may carry the Chinese characters of the station.
4. **Krahl, *China Missions in Crisis* (1964)** and a local gazetteer (《东西湖区志》/《武汉市志·宗教》) — Laimbeckhoven's Hou-koang years and the founding date of the Baiquan parish, to test the continuity between the 1737–1771 Jesuit station/oratory and the modern 柏泉天主堂 site.
