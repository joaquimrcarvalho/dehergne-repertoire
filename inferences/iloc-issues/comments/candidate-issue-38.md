### «in Tum cheu missionis Kim tum», au T'ong-tcheou du Hopei, pensons-nous, non à celui du Kiangsu — Inácio Francisco  ·  🟢 confident

`jesuita-votos-local`, **1767-11-04** (`deh-inacio-francisco`)

- **Place name (Chinese)**: 通州
- **Pinyin**: Tōngzhōu
- **Wikidata code**: **Q393836** — 通州区 Tongzhou District, Beijing (P625: 39.90858 N, 116.65133 E)
- **Historical counterpart**: Q15916190 — 通州, "历史上的顺天府通州" (the Tongzhou of Shuntian prefecture), explicitly *different from* the Jiangsu 通州直隸州 (P1889 → Q17007611)
- **Province (Qing)**: Zhili / Hopei 直隸 — today Beijing 北京市
- **Mission district in the formula**: `Kim tum` = 京東 Jīngdōng, "east of the capital" (inference — see §2)

## 1. What the source says

**Dehergne, *Répertoire des jésuites de Chine de 1552 à 1800* (1973), notice no. 334 — "Francisco (Francesco), Inácio (port.)", pp. 100–101** (the vow formula is on **p. 101**):

> «V. 4 nov. 1767 « in Tum cheu missionis Kim tum », au T'ong-tcheou du Hopei, pensons-nous, non à celui du Kiangsu, c. spir. (*Lus.* 24, 291): Ign. FRANCISCUS.»

Read literally: **final vows as *coadjutor spiritualis*, 4 November 1767, "at Tum cheu, of the Kim tum mission"**, entered in **ARSI, *Lusitania* 24, f. 291**, signed *Ign. Franciscus*. Dehergne's parenthesis — "at the T'ong-tcheou of Hopei, we think, not the one of Kiangsu" — is his own, and it is correct.

The person (same notice, **pp. 100–101**): Portuguese, born at Conraria, diocese of Coimbra, **14 or 21 August 1725** (from the tombstone); entered the China mission **24 August 1742**; priest *v.* 1750; embarked for China **1752** (W 2021 = Wicki, *Liste der Jesuiten-Indienfahrer*, no. 2021); arrived the same year with the Portuguese ambassador **Francisco Xavier de Assis Pacheco**; both at **Peking 1753**; **surgeon** (LSF, 189 = Lo-shu Fu, *A Documentary Chronicle of Sino-Western Relations*); died **Peking, 9 December 1792** (Planchet, p. 198). The notice's closing reference «**Pf. 869**» is a *printed page* of Pfister, not a notice number (see §6).

## 2. How the vow formula is built — "place + mission district"

The formula is the standard shape of the entries in that register: a place, then the mission district it belongs to. The same volume gives the pattern repeatedly:

| Vow formula (as printed by Dehergne) | Glossed by Dehergne as | Page |
|---|---|---|
| «Tun Kim in sacello S.J.» (6 Jan. 1638, Gaspar do Amaral) | "au Tonkin" | **p. 11** |
| «Sum Kiam, Kiam-nam» (14 Feb. 1768, José Correa Tcheou, no. 195) | Sungkiang, **Kiangnan** | **p. 61** |
| «superior missionis in Tumkim» (catalogue, on Abraham Le Royer) | "la mission du Tonkin" | **p. 150** |
| «Pekim» (8 Dec. 1704, João Domingos Paramino) | Peking | **p. 194** |
| «Xu le leu kia chuom» (7 June 1744, Joseph Saraiva) | Leou-kia-tchouang "du Chou-lou hien" | **p. 240** |
| «in oppido Hien (sic) missionis Tunkinensis» (24 Feb. 1695, Jacobus Vidal) | a town **of the Tonkin mission** | **p. 291** |

So `in Tum cheu missionis Kim tum` = **at Tum cheu, of the Kim tum mission**, with the second element naming a district (like *Kiangnan*, *Hukwang*, *Tonkin*), not a second town.

**The romanisation of the two elements.** In these registers 京 is **`Kim`** (the vow «Pekim» = 北京, p. 194) and 東 is **`Tum`/`Tun`** (東京 = *Tumkim*/*Tun Kim* = Tonkin, pp. 11 and 150; and, in a Latin work of the same tradition, `Tum Cheu` renders 東周 in Trigault, **lib. I, p. 4**). Read in that key, **`Kim tum` = 京 + 東 = 京東**, "east of the capital" — the district east of Peking whose town is Tongzhou, which is exactly what makes the formula coherent and what makes Dehergne's choice of the Hopei town the obvious one. **This reading is an inference:** Dehergne glosses `Tum cheu` and leaves `Kim tum` unexplained, and no source consulted names a *missio Kimtum*.

**The alternative, rejected.** `Kim tum` is 東京 reversed, and 東京 is Tonkin in the same registers. But Dehergne glosses `Tumkim`/`Tun Kim` as Tonkin on pp. 11 and 150 and does not do so here; Inácio Francisco never left the Peking region (Pfister, **p. 869**: "il demeura dans cette ville le reste de sa vie"); and no place in Tonkin is called *Tum cheu*. The transposition explanation is therefore unnecessary.

## 3. Dehergne identifies the place himself, in 1953 and 1957

**Dehergne, "Les chrétientés de Chine de la période Ming (1581–1650)", *Monumenta Serica* 16 (1957), p. 100, entry no. 15:**

> «**15. *Tungchow* (T'ong-tcheou) 通州**, port fluvial à **4 lieues de Peking, à l'est**. Actuel **Tunghsien 通縣**. *M.*: Tung; *A.*: Tom-tcheou. **Distinct du Tungchow (mêmes caractères) indépendant du Kiangsu; du Tungchow fou du Shensi; d'un Tungchow du Kweichow.**»

i.e. a **river port four leagues east of Peking**, in 1957 still called **通縣 Tunghsien**, and to be kept apart from a same-character 通州 in **Jiangsu**, a *fou* of **Shaanxi** (同州), and a fourth in **Kweichow**. The same entry adds the earliest trace of a Christian community there: *"Le Dr Paul Siu 徐 reste un mois entier à Tungchow en 1620: chrétienté probable"* (Paul Siu = 徐光啟 Xu Guangqi; the source is the *Annuelle* of 1621, where Dehergne notes the spelling `Tumcleun`).

The same town stands in Dehergne's earlier gazetteer of the Peking mission around 1700 — **Dehergne, "La mission de Pékin vers 1700 : étude de géographie missionnaire", *Archivum Historicum Societatis Iesu* 22 (1953), entry no. 40, printed p. 329**:

> «**40. Tungchow (T'ong-tcheou) 通州**. D'ANVILLE: Tom-tcheou, port fluvial à l'est de Pékin; actuel Tunghsien.»

The Shaanxi namesake has its own entry in the 1957 volume — **p. 113** (*"III. TUNGCHOW (T'ong-tcheou) 同州 fou au nord-est de Sian. Actuel Tali 大荔 …"*) — which repeats the warning about "d'autres Tungchow (non fou) au Hopeh et au Kiangsu".

## 6. Discrepancies, and what remains open

- **Dates.** Pfister (**t. 2, notice no. 401, p. 869**) heads the notice *"N. 15 août 1725. — E. 24 oct. 1742. — A. 1752. — M. 9 déc. 1792, Pékin"*, against Dehergne's 14 or 21 August 1725 and 24 August 1742 (pp. 100–101), and Pfister's footnote gives the tombstone age as "plutôt 84e" against the 80th year in his own text.
- **«Pf. 869».** Dehergne's abbreviation list (**p. XXXV**) states expressly: *"Le chiffre renvoie à la page, non au numéro de la notice"* — so 869 is the printed page, and the notice is Pfister's **no. 401**. Likewise «LSF, 189» is a page of Lo-shu Fu, and «W 2021» a number in Wicki's *Liste der Jesuiten-Indienfahrer* (defined **p. XXXVI**).
- **`Kim tum`** stays an inference (§2). The register itself, **ARSI *Lusitania* 24, f. 291**, was not consulted directly for this note.
- **No resident Jesuit at Tongzhou in 1767 is documented.** The 1701 residence list carries "*Tungchow* R'" — a residence without a priest in residence — under **PEKING (actuel HEBEI)** (**Dehergne 1973, p. 359**), and the Peking chronology line reads "*Tungchow? 1620*", question mark Dehergne's (**p. 355**). Pfister's statement that Francisco's ministry covered "les chrétiens de la capitale et des stations voisines" (p. 869) is the closest the printed record comes to a station; the vow formula is the only 18th-century text that puts him *there*.
- **His Chinese name** is given differently by the two authorities: Dehergne, p. 100 — *"Tchang Chou Yi-na (Pf.). Ki-Sien (LSF, 189: Chang Chi-Hsien)"*; Pfister, p. 869 — *"TCHANG CHOU I-NA"*. Unresolved here.
