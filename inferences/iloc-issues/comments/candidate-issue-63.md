## Archive research report (2026-10-14)

**Verdict in one line:** the village "Tong ko" is **not identified**; the district is fixed with good confidence — **Tong ko was a Christian village with an oratory ("église") near Changshu 常熟, Jiangsu** (today 常熟市, Suzhou; Wikidata **Q1015805**) — and there are strong signs that Dehergne's entry no. 401 **fused the arrest of d'Attimis (11 Dec 1747, at Tong ko) with the arrest of Henriques (21 Dec 1747, elsewhere)**.

### 1. The person's itinerary (compact)

António-José Henriques, Portuguese, b. Lisbon 13 June 1707; Chinese name Houang Ngan-to 黄安多 (Pf.). Attaché of the Metello de Sousa embassy; entered the Society at Macao 25 Dec 1727; studies at Manila; back at Macao 13 Oct 1737; destined for the **Nanjing (Kiangnan) mission**; superior of that mission (successor of Hinderer); vicar general of Nanjing 3 Nov 1744. Per the Beijing tribunal sentence (text reproduced in Rossi, *Documenta*, pp. 121–149), he worked in the prefectures of Suzhou 蘇州, Songjiang 松江, Taicang 太倉, Changzhou 常州, Zhenjiang 鎮江 (Jiangsu), Jiaxing 嘉興 (Zhejiang) and Huizhou 徽州 (Anhui); in 1740 at Danyang 丹陽 (in the Wang family); in 1743 at **Changshu 常熟** (in the Chen and Tang families); he lived near Changshu, in a residence built by P. Ho or in the Ts'ien family (Pfister, t. 2, printed p. 706). Arrested 21 Dec 1747; tried and condemned to death 14 Feb 1748; strangled at Suzhou in the night of 13–14 Aug 1748 (Dehergne; Pfister gives 12 Sept 1748).

### 2. The attestations, with printed pages

**Dehergne, *Répertoire des Jésuites de Chine* (1973), no. 401 (Henriques), printed p. 124:**

> « arrêté le 21 déc. 1747 à **Tongko** près de « **Tsangtzo** » dans la **maison de Philippe Wang** (entre Soochow et Kashing (Kia-hing fou); procès, 14 févr. 1748 et condamnation à mort (**M 444, 339-356**; interrogatoire, **M 444, 427-456**) »

"M" = Archives des Missions Étrangères de Paris (Dehergne's own siglum list, printed p. XXXIV) — **not consulted**.

**Dehergne, *Répertoire* (1973), no. 51 (Tristano Francesco d'Attimis / "de Athemis"), printed p. 17** — the same place-name in the author's own identification:

> « capturé le 11 déc. 1747 durant son action de grâces dans **l'église de Tong ko, près de « Tzang zo » (Tch'ang-chou, Changshu)** »

This is the decisive cross-identification: **Dehergne himself glosses "Tzang zo" = Tch'ang-chou = Changshu 常熟**. The Wu-dialect pronunciation of 常熟 (≈ */zaŋ zoʔ/*) is exactly what a European ear writes "Tsang-tzo/Tzang zo" — so the gloss is also phonetically sound (the parenthetical gloss is Dehergne's; the phonetic reading of the quoted form is my inference).

**Pfister, *Notices*, t. 2, notice no. 322 (Le P. Antoine-Joseph Henriques), printed pp. 705–710** — the full narrative from the *Lettres édifiantes* (Panthéon ed., t. III, pp. 825 seq.), the *Welt-Bott* (nos 685, 693, 694) and the same Beijing tribunal text:

- **p. 706:** Henriques lived near **Tch'ang-chou 常熟**; the tribunal text lists his prefectures (above); the degraded bachelier **Siu** owned the farm ("grange") near the P. Ho residence.
- **p. 707:** the accusation was received by the Grand Judge **Wong-tsao (王藻)** at **Tch'ang-chou**; Henriques had gone to Tch'ang-chou to confer with P. de Athemis; his boat was recognised.
- **p. 708:** the apostate catechist **Charles Siu** led the soldiers to **Philippe Wang's house**; the servant said "the Father is here" — it was **P. de Athemis, of whom Philippe [Wang] was the catechist**; de Athemis was seized during his thanksgiving (i.e. in the house-oratory = "l'église de Tong ko" of Dehergne p. 17), with the Christian Joseph T'ang; Philippe Wang arrested soon after — **11 Dec 1747**. Pfister's n. 3 adds that, per the tribunal-des-Peines text, de Athemis was arrested "non pas dans cette famille Wang" but in another family.
- **p. 709:** Henriques escaped, was tracked **"de Tch'ang-chou à Sou-tcheou, et de Sou-tcheou à Kia-hing"**; **the Song family**, sheltering him and the catechist **Paul Diego Madeira**, was stormed; both were seized **21 Dec 1747** and taken in chains to Suzhou.

So the printed sources describe **two distinct arrests**: de Athemis on 11 Dec at Tong ko (Philippe Wang's house, near Changshu), Henriques on 21 Dec at the Song family house **on the Suzhou–Jiaxing stretch** of his flight. Dehergne's entry 401 attaches the *date* of the second to the *place* of the first, and adds the flight-route locator "(entre Soochow et Kashing)". Whether the MEP dossier M 444 itself already conflates them cannot be checked from the printed sources consulted.

Geographic sanity check (haversine from the old Suzhou city seat, 31.310 N 120.618 E): Changshu lies **40 km at 17° (NNW)** of Suzhou; Jiaxing lies **63 km at 168° (S)**. Changshu is thus *not* "between Soochow and Kashing" — the entry's parenthesis cannot be a gloss of "Tsangtzo"; it must reflect the flight narrative (or the MEP dossier's own wording). This confirms the tension rather than resolving it.

**Dehergne, « Les chrétientés de Chine de la période Ming (1581–1650) », *Monumenta Serica* 16 (1957)** was checked for the district's stations: it covers only 1581–1650, and its only "Tong-ko" hits are 東莞 Tong-koan, Guangdong (printed pp. 84, 90, 118, 135) — irrelevant here.

### 4. Verdict

**🟡 district-level anchor only; village not identified.**

- **Tong ko = a Christian village with an oratory/church, the house of the catechist Philippe Wang, in the Changshu 常熟 region (Suzhou prefecture, Jiangsu)** — on Dehergne's own cross-entry gloss (entry 51, printed p. 17: «l'église de Tong ko, près de "Tzang zo" (Tch'ang-chou, Changshu)»), corroborated by Pfister's narrative (all 11 Dec events unfold at/near Tch'ang-chou: the judge 王藻 sits there, the Siu/Ts'ien/Philippe Wang households are of that région). District anchor for the project: **常熟市 Changshu (county-level city, Suzhou, Jiangsu) = Q1015805** (P442 `32 05 81`; P131 → Suzhou); historically 常熟縣, 蘇州府. The candidate Chinese reading fielded in the issue ("？") has no printed support for the village and should stay empty.
- **The village itself: not identified.** No Chinese characters for "Tong ko" survive in the consulted printed sources, no gazetteer match is possible from the bare romanisation, and generic homophone items in Wikidata are geographically excluded. Per the honesty rules, a guess (東郭/同戈/塘角…) would be worse than "not identified".
- **Data-quality flag (inference, labelled as such):** the project's `estadia` line itself is probably misplaced — as to *place* it describes the **11 Dec arrest of d'Attimis**; Henriques was seized on 21 Dec 1747 **elsewhere**, in the Song family house on the Suzhou→Jiaxing stretch (Pfister, printed pp. 708–709). Dehergne 1973 no. 401 (p. 124) fuses the two events. If the team prefers to field what the printed evidence supports for Henriques himself on that date, the event would move to "between Soochow and Kia-hing (Song family house)" — itself not identifiable below district level from these sources.

### 5. What would settle it

1. **AMEP, vol. 444, ff. 339–356 (procès) and 427–456 (interrogatoire)** — Dehergne's cited source; it should give the arrest place(s) verbatim, with any Chinese characters, and show whether the fusion is Dehergne's or the dossier's.
2. **Rossi, Gabriel. *Documenta circa vitam et martyrium PP. Henriquez et de Athemis*. T'ou-sè-wè, 1891, pp. 121–149** — the Chinese text of the Beijing sentence (江南天主邪教案) with a certified Latin translation; it names the arrest village and will carry the **characters for "Tong ko" and "Tsang zo"**.
3. The **Lettres édifiantes** (Panthéon ed., t. III, pp. 825–830) and **Der Neue Welt-Bott** nos 693–694 — the eyewitness letters behind Pfister's narrative.
4. Local gazetteer work: a list of Christian villages with churches around Changshu c. 1740 (江南传教史 literature) to match a name pronounced /doŋ koʔ/ (candidates: 東×家/×角/×谷 — speculative, unattested).
