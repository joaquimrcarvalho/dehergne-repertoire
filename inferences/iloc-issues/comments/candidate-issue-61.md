## Identification report (14 October 2026)

### 1. The person's itinerary (compact)

Gottfried Xaver von Laimbeckhoven, ch. name Nan Houai-jen 南懷仁 (same as Verbiest) / Ngo-tô (Pfister: NGO-TÉ), Austrian, b. Vienna 1707-01-09; entered the Society Vienna 1722-01-27; embarked Lisbon 1736-04-25 (W 1837); nearly a year in Mozambique; Goa; Macao 1738-08-05; Hukwang (Huguang) from 1739-03; 4th vows 1740-02-02 at «Pe tsien schan» (30 km from Hankow); Visitor of Japan and China 1746, exercising 1748–1751; Macao 1749-01; Beijing 1749-12; preconized Bishop of Nanjing 1752, consecrated at Macao 1755-07-22; administrator of the Beijing diocese 1757-08-31–1780; persecution at Luyi hien (Honan) 1771; then residing in his Nanjing diocese: Changchow fou 1771, Wouho 1772, Tsungming island 1778-11-13, Nanjing 1780, **Sungkiang 1781**; died **1787-05-22 at T'ang-kia-hiang**; body carried to Sou-tcheou (Suzhou) and buried in the Christians' cemetery at Pé-ho-chan; funerary monument erected by the new Kiangnan mission in 1877 (Pfister, t. 2, p. 770).

### 2. Findings

**Dehergne 1973, entry no. 446** (entry heading «Laimbeckhoven, Gottfried Xaver von (autrich.) Mgr 446», printed **pp. 141–142**). The death line, verbatim (printed p. 142):

> «M. 22 mai 1787, T'ang-kia-hiang (en dialecte: Daong-ka-hang ») près de Sungkiang, Kiangnan. Mgr de Gouvea dit: le 20 mai (KRAHL, 301). Pf. 760; EC (Cel. TESTORE).»

Dehergne cites **Pf. 760** — the head of Pfister's notice 352.

**Pfister, Notices, t. 2, notice 352 «Mgr Godefroid-Xavier de Laimbeckhoven»** (printed **pp. 760–770**):

- p. 760 (notice head): «M. 22 † mai 1787, T'ang-kia-Hhiang.»
- p. 768: in 1785, exactly a century after Mgr Grégoire Lopez's first episcopal visit (1685), he «visita toutes les chrétientés où il put aller sans exposer les chrétiens», 15 names kept — his final circuit of the Kiangnan chrétientés.
- **p. 769 (the death narrative, verbatim):** «Mgr Godefroid-Xavier de Laimbeckhoven, sentant venir son heure dernière, se retira dans **la petite chrétienté de T'ang-kia-hiang 湯家巷/唐家巷**, **non loin de Song-kiang et des bords du Hoang-p'ou**; souhaitant la bienvenue à la mort qui approchait, il remit son âme à son Dieu **le 22 mai 1787**… Le P. Jean Yao 姚 arriva à temps pour l'assister…». Footnote: «L'abbé Pierre, *Vie de Mgr Languillat*, t. II, p. 227, note 1: ‹mort en 1787, à **Tong-Ka-Haong**› (sic)» — another rendering of the same dialect name; the 1877 tomb inscription reads «IX Jun. MDCCLXXXVII» (editor's note).
- p. 770: body carried to Sou-tcheou, buried at the cemetery of Pé-ho-chan; monument 1877.

**Pfister, t. 2, notice 322 (P. Ant.-Joseph Henriques), printed p. 715:** «Des chrétiens de **T'ang-kia-hiang 唐家巷, au P'ou-tong 浦東**, l'ayant su, vinrent secrètement les chercher…» — a chrétienté of that exact name located **in Pudong** (the region east of the Huangpu, opposite Shanghai; Pfister elsewhere glosses «P'ou-tong… région à l'est de Chang-hai», p. 984).

**Pfister, t. 2, Table alphabétique, printed p. 1092 — the author's own cross-identification (decisive):**

> «**T'ang-kia-hiang 唐家巷** («T'aong-ka-haong»), **au P'ou-tong**: tradition sur Henriques et de Attimis, 715, — **Mgr de Laimbeckhoven y meurt, 769**.»

Pfister's index knows ONE T'ang-kia-hiang — in Pudong — and assigns both the Henriques/Attimis tradition (p. 715) and Laimbeckhoven's death (p. 769) to it.

**Backer-Sommervogel, t. 6, cols 1305–1306** (Puntscher/Purger block): a late-19th-century Jesuit (went to China 1875, taught at Zi-ka-wei) «mourut à **Tang-kia-hiang**, le 28 septembre 1890» — the chrétienté was still a Jesuit residence a century later.

**Modern derivative:** catholic-hierarchy.org gives death place «**Tong-kia-hang**» (the dialect form; no characters).

### 4. Chinese toponym

**唐家巷 Tángjiāxiàng** «Tang-family lane» — not 唐家乡 (township) and not 唐家行. The final character is printed **巷** on all three pages (715, 769, 1092), and Dehergne's dialect form settles it phonetically: Shanghainese 巷 is /ɦɑ̃/ ≈ «-hang», exactly the third syllable of «Daong-ka-hang» / «T'aong-ka-haong» / «Tong-kia-hang» (乡 would be /ɕiã/ «-shiang»). The reviewer's 唐家乡 is therefore excluded by the sources' own characters and dialect spelling. The first character, however, is **not settled by the printed book**: p. 769 prints 湯家巷 while pp. 715 and 1092 print 唐家巷; the two surnames are both «Tang» in transcription, and the dialect form (voiced *Daong-*) favours 唐.

**Geography.** Pfister's index places the chrétienté **au P'ou-tong** — Pudong, east of the Huangpu, i.e. in the Qing **Songjiang prefecture (松江府)**, which then administered Shanghai county and the Pudong bank. His narrative phrase «non loin de Song-kiang et des bords du Hoang-p'ou» must be read regionally (prefecture, Huangpu banks): the Songjiang prefecture seat (31.0095, 121.1078) lies **47.5 km at 60° (ENE)** from the Pudong New Area centroid (55.7 km from Tangzhen) — too far for «near the city», exact for «east bank of the Huangpu, Songjiang prefecture». Dehergne's looser «près de Sungkiang, Kiangnan» drops the Pudong detail; the reviewer's «near Sungkiang» inference followed Dehergne and missed Pfister's index.

### 5. VERDICT — 🟡 tentative (prefecture-level anchor; village itself not identified)

- **What is established (printed sources):** the death place is the **chrétienté 唐家巷 (Wu: Daong-ka-hang), in Pudong, Songjiang prefecture (松江府), Kiangnan** — today's Pudong New Area, Shanghai. Author's own identification: Pfister index, p. 1092.
- **What is wrong in the reviewer's proposal:** (a) 唐家乡 misreads the printed 巷 (a lane/village name, not a township); (b) "near Sungkiang" mislocates it — the village is not near Songjiang city but across the Huangpu, in Pudong; (c) **Q11104671 is 松江府 Songjiang Fu**, the *historical prefecture* (31.0095, 121.1078), not the modern Songjiang district — as an anchor it is actually of the right administrative level for 1787, but it is a prefecture-level, not a village-level, identification.
- **Wikidata check (read 2026-10-14):** no item exists for a Pudong/Shanghai 唐家巷. wbsearchentities 唐家巷 → Q13992701 (Sichuan), Q13811722 (唐家巷社区, old Suzhou city), Q13756716 (Shaanxi); SPARQL 唐家* under Shanghai (Q8686) → only 唐家沙/唐家苑/唐家湾 communities. Q11104671 has no P150 (containment cannot be verified both ways on Wikidata). Modern region anchor: **Q125378 浦东新区** Pudong New Area (31.2231, 121.5397; P131 = Q8686). Name collision caveat applies in full: 唐家-toponyms are everywhere, and Pudong's 唐镇 (from 唐墓橋) is a *different* name that must not be substituted.
- **Net:** 🟡 — keep a **prefecture-level anchor Q11104671 (松江府)** with the comment corrected to «au P'ou-tong (Pudong), Songkiang fu», or move to **Q125378 (Pudong New Area)** as the modern regional anchor; do **not** use 唐家乡, and do not point at the modern 松江区. A 🟢 would require a village-level identification.

### 6. What would settle it

1. **Local gazetteers**: 《川沙县志》/《上海县志》/《南汇县志》 natural-village lists for a 唐家巷 on the Pudong side (the 1787 village fell in Shanghai or Nanhui county; Chuansha 厅 was only created in 1801).
2. **Krahl 1964** (*China Missions in Crisis. Bishop Laimbeckhoven and his Times*, Analecta Gregoriana 137, Rome 1964 — already cited by Dehergne as «KRAHL, 301» for the death date), which builds on de Gouvea's relatio and may give the precise location and characters.
3. **Zikawei MSS**: Pfister's sources for the death narrative — the 1877 tomb dossier and P. Pierre Hoang's unpublished *Histoire de la chrétienté de Sou-tcheou* — would also settle the 唐/湯 printing discrepancy.
4. Creating a Wikidata item for the historical chrétienté 唐家巷 (Pudong) is only worth doing once 1–3 fix which modern settlement, if any, continues it.
